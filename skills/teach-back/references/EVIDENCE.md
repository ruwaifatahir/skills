# Evidence behind teach-back

Three papers.

## Bisra, Liu, Nesbit, Salimi & Winne 2018, Inducing Self-Explanation

Educational Psychology Review. Meta analysis, 69 effect sizes from 64 reports,
5,917 participants. Learners prompted to self explain versus learners not
prompted.

| Comparison | g |
| --- | --- |
| Overall | 0.55, CI [0.45, 0.65] |
| Self explanation vs an instructional explanation | 0.35 |
| Self explanation vs no additional explanation | 0.67 |
| Multiple choice prompts | 0.24, not distinguishable from zero (k = 2) |
| Metacognitive prompts, explain your planning | not distinguishable from zero |
| Anticipatory prompts, predict the next step and say why | 1.37 (k = 1, treat with caution) |

Heterogeneity I2 = 65%. For scale, the authors put 0.55 alongside mastery
learning (0.50) and peer tutoring (0.55) in Hattie's review.

What each row is doing to this skill:

- **The gate is an open question, never options.** Multiple choice was the one
  prompt format that did not work. The authors read it as cue strength: the
  options do the retrieval for the learner. Only two studies, so it is weak
  evidence on its own.
- **Ask before you explain.** Generating an explanation beat receiving one,
  0.35. Schworm and Renkl found that learners given self explanation prompts
  plus instructional explanations on demand did worse than learners given the
  prompts alone. Availability removes the incentive to work. That is why the
  skill teaches, then asks, then waits.
- **Ask about the content, not about their process.** Conceptual prompts were
  reliably effective. Metacognitive prompts, the "how well do you think you
  understand this" kind, were not.
- **Format past that barely matters.** Question or instruction, during or
  after, specific or general: post hoc tests found no significant differences.
  So the skill fixes what the evidence fixes and leaves the rest to judgment.
- **Revision is the strong move.** The authors' closing point: the most
  powerful application may be an initial explanation followed by a prompt to
  revise it once new information exposes a gap. That is step 7's cue and retry.

## Kobayashi 2019, Learning by Preparing-to-Teach and Teaching

Japanese Psychological Research 61(3), 192-203. Meta analysis, 28 studies,
against studying with no teaching expectancy.

| Condition | g |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared to teach, did not teach | 0.35, CI [0.27, 0.44] |
| Prepared to teach and taught | 0.56 |
| Prepare only, deep learning | 0.31 |
| Prepare and teach, deep learning | 0.55 |
| Prepare only, immediate test | 0.28 |
| Prepare only, delayed test | 0.53 |
| Teaching, interactive | 0.84, CI [0.75, 0.93] (k = 4) |
| Teaching, non interactive | 0.48, CI [0.42, 0.53] (k = 12) |
| Expecting interactive teaching | 0.50 |
| Expecting non interactive teaching | 0.27 |

What it is doing to this skill:

- **Announce the check up front.** Expectancy alone, with no teaching at all,
  was worth 0.35, and it held better at a delay (0.53) than immediately (0.28).
  One sentence at the start of the session buys that.
- **Keep it a conversation.** Interactive teaching nearly doubled the effect of
  a one shot explanation with no exchange. The skill asks, responds, and asks
  again rather than requesting one long write up.
- **Where it is thin.** Heterogeneity was 76 to 94%. Delayed tests were all
  within one week. Almost all samples were 18 and over, and for school age
  samples the preparing to teach effect was 0.04, not different from zero.

## Kobayashi 2022, The Retrieval Practice Hypothesis in Research on Learning by Teaching

Frontiers in Psychology 13:842668. Mini review. Asks whether the benefit of
explaining to others is entirely the testing effect, and concludes the evidence
cannot yet answer it.

Why the skill reads it anyway:

- **Written explaining is the weak case.** Benefits of explaining to others show
  up when explanations are oral and often not when they are written
  (Hoogerheide et al. 2016; Lachner et al. 2021b meta analysis). A chat skill is
  written. This is why the gate is built on self explanation, where the evidence
  is written friendly, rather than on teach back to an imagined student.
- **Closed book, with feedback.** More retrieval effort produces a larger effect,
  as long as failure gets corrected. Open book explaining lets the learner lean
  on the text and spend less effort. So the skill asks with the page closed and
  always reveals after the attempt.
- **Elaboration predicts learning.** Learners who add bridging inferences,
  examples, and analogies beyond the material learn more (Roscoe and Chi 2008;
  Fiorella and Kuhlmann 2020). That is why step 6 asks for one example of their
  own, not just a restatement.

## What this skill deliberately does not claim

The mechanism is unsettled. Kobayashi 2022 finds the retrieval practice
hypothesis untested on all three of its own tests. The skill copies the effects
that replicate, not the explanation for them.

The studies cover learners working through instructional material: text,
worked examples, and lessons. The skill inherits that scope, which is why it
turns away reference lists and material that is already a quiz.
